# ADR-001: Adoption of a Polyglot-Persistence, Microservice-Oriented Architecture for FitFlow

**Status:** Accepted

## Context
FitFlow needs to support real-time social features, AI-driven personalization, camera-based nutrition logging, and strict handling of sensitive health data, while being built and maintained by a mid-sized engineering team on a startup budget and timeline (per the case study's emphasis on speed-to-market and cost-effectiveness).

## Decision
Adopt:
- **React Native** for the client (mobile + web)
- **NestJS** core API combined with a separate **FastAPI** AI microservice
- **PostgreSQL** as the system of record, with **Firebase Firestore/RTDB** for real-time data
- **Redis** for caching
- **Firebase Authentication** for identity

## Alternatives Considered
- **Fully native (Swift + Kotlin) build** — rejected due to duplicated effort and cost across iOS/Android.
- **Kotlin Multiplatform + Go + DynamoDB + Cognito** — rejected because, while performant and scalable, it scored lower on development speed, cost, and team familiarity in the weighted decision matrix (see `comparison-matrix.md`).
- **Single-database approach (PostgreSQL-only or Firebase-only)** — rejected because neither store alone satisfies both the transactional-integrity needs of health data and the low-latency needs of real-time social features.

## Consequences
- The team must maintain two data stores (PostgreSQL and Firebase) and keep them consistent for social-feed data, adding some operational complexity — offset by significantly better fit for FitFlow's mixed workload.
- Separating the AI microservice allows the data-science team to iterate on models independently without redeploying the core API, at the cost of an additional internal network hop for AI-dependent requests.
